The scenarios

A scenario is a JSON file in tools/sim/scenarios/: how many members, what they do, and what the network does to them (Scenario format). The creator is saro, then come raya, pax, mira, kes, tomo, and peer7 on. Each scenario CI runs has a job:

ScenarioDoesRun by
grow-waku-loss-*.jsonthe goal: 200 members on Waku’s delays, up to three adders a round, toward epoch 50, at a rate of loss the name gives in percentlogos-chat’s pull requests at 0.5, 1 and 5%, and its main at those and 0%; chat-sim’s CI at each; the long run at 0.1%
50-epochs-by-saro-waku-loss-*.json51 members, saro alone adding one a round, toward epoch 50chat-sim’s CI, and logos-chat main’s long run

three.json3 members seated, one post, on fast timersyour first run

Each grow-waku-loss- and 50-epochs-by-saro-waku-loss- file has a -groupv3 copy, the same on a GroupV3 group. Every other scenario CI runs sets the library’s own timers ("timers": "default"), as logos-chat-module runs a group. A GroupV3 group keeps timers of its own, whatever the file says.

A group that grows

Section titled “A group that grows”

A grow step is the scenario’s only step. Each round, it draws one to adders members, and each adds the next member at once, on whatever branch it holds. With by in place of adders, that member alone adds, one a round. A round ends when each member added holds the group and no member that was level with its branch at the start is behind it, or when the budget runs out.

A member is behind when it holds a state another member has gone past. It is then waited on no more and drawn no more until it catches up. An adder refused all round is drawn no more until it holds another state. The draws come from the scenario’s seed.

The states the members hold make lines. The creator’s first state is on the trunk. Where a state has two or more after it, the one more members hold or have gone past carries the line on, and each other starts a branch. A branch off the trunk is a main branch, and one off any other branch a sub-branch. With by, the adder’s own line is the trunk.

The step ends at the start of a round once the trunk holds epochs, the trunk and the main branches number branches or more, every member has been seated, or no member can be drawn. With size, an adder whose add would take the group above size first removes the member seated longest ago. Here is how one run of grow-waku-loss-0.5.json ended, as its run.txt prints it:

Its first fork is at epoch 5, where line 2 left the trunk, so it went 5 epochs without a fork. The run ended when the trunk and four main branches made five.

The goal

Section titled “The goal”

The grow-waku-loss- scenarios ask how many epochs a growing group lasts before it first forks. Each part of the file has a reason:

  • Up to three adders a round, on any branch. Two adders can commit for one epoch, which a GroupV3 group needs to fork. Members on a branch keep adding, as members of a real group would.
  • Toward epoch 50. A run that never forks goes 50 epochs without a fork: the goal.
  • Five branches. A run goes on past its first fork, and ends once the trunk and four main branches run side by side. That bounds a run that forks early.
  • 200 members. The step ends once every member is seated, so there is a member for each add on the way to epoch 50.
  • The same loss for Welcomes. Each member loses that share of the frames it receives, Welcomes among them. A joiner whose Welcome is lost never joins.
  • Waku’s delays. Each member receives every frame late, by a delay of its own (below).

The rates are chosen for what a run can show. At 0% a fork is the code’s alone, and it is the slowest rate to run, so main runs it on three seeds and a pull request not at all. At 0.5, 1 and 5%, GroupV3 forks on each of seeds 0 to 2, so a change in when it forks can show. At 0.1% two of those seeds stay whole.

At the revision chat-sim pins, about half of the members added to a GroupV3 group never join, even at no loss: of a round’s adds, one is applied (How a run works).

Waku’s delays

Section titled “Waku’s delays”

The delays come from the relay simulation the Waku team published as rln-delay-simulations in logos-messaging/research (#42). It ran 1000 nwaku nodes in Shadow, each with 6 peers in its mesh and 100 ms on each link (shadow.yaml), and lost no packet. Its 10 KB messages gave 8991 arrivals, from 263 to 673 ms, with a median of 518 ms (raw/latency_10kb.txt).

A scenario gives each member one of those arrivals’ quantiles, in a fixed order, and gives saro the median. Its 200 members get 268 to 659 ms, the 51 of a fifty-epochs scenario 284 to 644 ms, and the 3001 of grow-waku-size-50.json 263 to 673 ms. A member’s delay is the same for every frame it receives, where on Waku it changes from message to message. No measured rate of loss on the live network was found, so the rates of loss are assumed.

The other scenarios

Section titled “The other scenarios”
  • 50-epochs-by-saro-waku-loss- has saro alone add, one member a round, with branches at 51, so a run goes on past any fork. One adder takes the race between adders out of a run, so what parts a GroupV2 group is what the network loses. With one committer a GroupV3 group cannot fork: a member that misses a commit falls behind on the same line.
  • grow-waku-size-50.json keeps the group at 50 members: each add past 50 first removes the member seated longest ago. It runs toward epoch 1000 and ends at its first main branch (branches 2). It runs on GroupV2 alone, since a GroupV3 group removes no member. It is the longest job of the long run.
  • 50-epochs.json and 50-epochs-waku.json seat 51 members, saro first and then 50 more one at a time, and have the last one post, the second on Waku’s delays. Each epoch after the first takes 70 s: 10 s to vote and the 60 s window. Their logs go to tools/forks, which must find the group whole.
  • three.json seats three members and has one post: a run of under a second, to try the tools on.

tools/sim/expected/ holds what each run of the files chat-sim’s CI sweeps came to at the revision of logos-chat that chat-sim pins (CONTRIBUTING.md).
